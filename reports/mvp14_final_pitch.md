# The Final 5-Minute Portfolio Pitch
## Structured Presentation Script for Technical & Front-Office Interviews

**Target Response Time**: Exactly 5 Minutes  
**Prompt**: *"So, tell me about your basketball analytics project and what it demonstrates."*  

---

### [0:00–0:30] Who I Am & The Core Problem
- **Verbal**: *"I am a basketball data analyst who bridges data engineering, statistical modeling, and on-court coaching decisions. The core problem I wanted to solve is that modern basketball staffs face an overload of noisy data—boxscores fluctuate wildly due to short tournament samples, and models often act as opaque black boxes that pretend to replace human judgment. I built an end-to-end analytical system to demonstrate how an analyst transforms raw data into clean, uncertainty-aware evidence that actively supports coaching staff decisions."*

---

### [0:30–1:30] What I Built
- **Verbal**: *"I built a modular analytics pipeline covering 20 years of international basketball across 18 tournaments, 1,145 games, and over 27,000 player performances. It is powered by a DuckDB relational warehouse with SHA-256 raw provenance, functional player role taxonomies, double-coded tactical video film ($\kappa = 0.80$), expanding 17-fold chronological machine learning ($\text{ECE} = 0.0314$), and 180,000 Monte Carlo simulations. The pipeline feeds an interactive Streamlit Analyst Workspace that strictly isolates pre-game information to eliminate hindsight bias and automatically surfaces tactical contradictions between stats and video."*

---

### [1:30–2:30] One Concrete Basketball Question
- **Verbal**: *"Let's take a concrete question: Heading into the Beijing 2008 Olympic Final against the USA 'Redeem Team', Spain had lost by 37 points in group play. The coaching question was: 'How can we maximize our win probability against an opponent that dominates fast-break transition off turnovers without falling into their defensive traps?'"*

---

### [2:30–3:30] How the Analysis Answered It
- **Verbal**: *"Our expanding walk-forward model showed USA favored at 73.2% with an 8.5-point expected margin. But our Contradiction Engine highlighted a vital clash: USA dominated transition, but Spain held a +4.2 Net Rating advantage in half-court paint efficiency. Furthermore, video coding showed USA's bigs were vulnerable in drop coverage against pick-and-pop perimeter trailers. Rather than dictating tactics, our Coaching Brief asked: (1) Can we deploy a 2-3 zone to break USA's transition flow? and (2) Can Pau and Marc Gasol punish USA's drop coverage by popping to the 3-point line? In reality, Spain executed those exact adjustments, cutting the deficit to 4 points with 2 minutes left in a competitive 118-107 game."*

---

### [3:30–4:15] What the Analysis Could NOT Tell Me (Humility & Limits)
- **Verbal**: *"Crucially, I acknowledge what data cannot do. A pre-game model cannot predict hot in-game shooting streaks, referee whistle thresholds, or locker-room emotional intensity. Feature importances describe historical associations, not guaranteed causal levers. An analyst must communicate these error bars honestly so coaches understand that a 70% win probability still means a 30% chance of an upset."*

---

### [4:15–5:00] How This Translates to Working Inside a Club
- **Verbal**: *"This translates directly to how I would operate on Day 1 in your organization: I listen first to your coaching philosophy, connect to your live data feeds, automate repeatable 2-page pre-game scouting briefs, and highlight tactical contradictions before film sessions. My goal is simple: to make your coaching staff faster, better informed, and more confident in every tactical decision."*
